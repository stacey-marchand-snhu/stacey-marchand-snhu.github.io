## Key Skills

Several areas are crucial to me as a professional developer:

### Collaboration

Throughout my entire career, the ability to collaborate within a team environment stands out most critically to make or break the success of projects and team longevity. The ability to cohesively function as a group amongst groups, distribute and parallelize work, drive innovative thinking, and organize together is what sets truly successful companies apart from the rest. In every collaborative work, I have always seen it as my responsibility to help drive the team forward, learning and mentoring as we share in the development and lessons that every job entails. Today, I work with an Agile squad, delivering software at a sustainable cadence and working together with star teammates to build a product that has immense value for our customers.

### Communication

As an engineer, stakeholders are always at the center of my work. I have always been a strong advocate for effective communication, and I have been fortunate to have had the opportunity to work with many talented individuals in the industry. I have learned that the ability to clearly articulate technical concepts, design decisions, and project status to both technical and non-technical audiences is essential for building trust, aligning expectations, and ensuring that the work we do has a meaningful impact. Whether it's through documentation, presentations, or one-on-one conversations, I strive to communicate in a way that is transparent, concise, and tailored to the needs of my audience.

### Data Structures & Algorithms

These are what make software development so powerful. This is where I have learned the importance of thinking algorithmically and designing solutions that are just the right balance of efficiency and elegance. I have been fortunate to expand my knowledge into neural networks and machine learning, advanced mathematics, and modern algorithmic techniques for searching, sorting, and analyzing.

### Software Design & Engineering

Software design and engineering are where technical skill becomes long-term professional value. Throughout the program, I have become more intentional about building software that is organized, maintainable, and designed with both users and future developers in mind. Every CS course emphasized the importance of clean architecture, reusable components, clear separation of concerns, and thoughtful interface design. This is the work that turns a project from a one-off prototype into a polished, professional application that can evolve and grow over time.

### Databases

Databases have been a major area of growth because they directly shape how reliable, useful, and scalable an application can become. Through projects involving relational concepts, MongoDB, Firebase Authentication, and Firestore, I gained stronger experience designing data around application needs, access patterns, and security requirements. I have learned to think carefully about how records are stored, retrieved, updated, and protected, especially when separating authentication concerns from user profile data or designing role-based access for administrative features.

### Security

Finally, security is one of the professional values I now see as inseparable from good software development. My coursework reinforced that security should not be treated as an afterthought, but as something built into the structure of an application from the beginning. Authentication, authorization, input validation, least-privilege access, privacy, and secure database rules all influence whether users and organizations can trust a system. It is paramount, unavoidable, and the cornerstone for safety, confidence, and longevity.

### **Artifact One**: CS-465 Full Stack Development - Travlr Website

![CS-465 App Changes](/assets/images/CS465-app-change.png)

This is a MEAN stack travel application with a Handlebars-based customer site and an Angular administrative interface backed by MongoDB. I enhanced this artifact in two ways:
- For the **Software Design & Engineering** category, I completely rewrote the administrative interface using React and TypeScript, migrating from the older Angular implementation. This rewrite introduced reusable components, a vastly improved modern UI, consolidated form logic, and a fully security-hardened authentication system. Identified vulnerabilities (including a broken registration error handler, inconsistent API response shapes, incorrect HTTP status codes, raw internal error objects returned to the client, and JWT tokens stored in localStorage) were all resolved. Tokens are now issued as httpOnly cookies, and all administrative routes are protected by a dedicated ProtectedRoute component.<br>
  **[Read the complete narrative for Enhancement One →](/enhancement-one-narrative/)**

- For the **Databases** category, I migrated the persistence layer from MongoDB to Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore, implementing typed service layers, least-privilege security rules, and role-based access control. Passwords are never stored in Firestore; user identity is handled entirely by Firebase Authentication, while only necessary profile and role metadata lives in a separate Firestore users collection.

### **Artifact Two**: CS-360 Android Application - HopStock

![CS-360 App Changes](/assets/images/hopstock-app-change.png)

This is the warehouse stock management Android application I developed from scratch in CS-360.
- For the **Data Structures & Algorithms** category, I enhanced the app by adding timestamped stock movement history stored in a new Room database table, a graph screen for each item that plots stock levels over time, and a linear regression algorithm that calculates a best-fit trend line and estimates when an item's stock level may reach zero. This transforms the original inventory tracker into a lightweight decision-support tool that gives warehouse staff and managers predictive insight into restocking needs.
